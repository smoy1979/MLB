Write a complete, production-ready Python script that pulls MLB team/venue metadata and solves an optimal league-wide regular season schedule for all 30 teams using Google OR-Tools CP-SAT.

1. Data Ingestion & Setup:
- Fetch active MLB teams, venues, geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude), and division alignment from the MLB Stats API:
  Endpoint: https://statsapi.mlb.com/api/v1/teams?sportId=1&hydrate=venue(location)
- Include hardcoded fallback metadata for all 30 MLB teams (team name, league, division, venue name, lat/lon coordinates) so the script can execute completely offline if the endpoint fails.
- Build a symmetric 30x30 great-circle distance matrix (in miles) using the Haversine formula.

2. Calendar Structure & Time Slot Architecture:
- Season duration: Assume target season runs March 25 to September 26 (~26 weeks).
- All-Star Break: Blackout July 12 through July 15 (All-Star Game on July 13).
- Model the schedule at the SERIES level first, then expand to daily games:
  * Divide the season into ~52 distinct Series Slots (Slot A: Midweek; Slot B: Weekend).
  * Standard Midweek series run Tuesday–Thursday (Monday off/travel).
  * Standard Weekend series run Friday–Sunday (Thursday off/travel).
  * Mondays and Thursdays are allowed to have games if necessary to fit the schedule.  In this case, schedule the day games when a team needs to travel to the next city.
  * Holiday Exceptions: On Memorial Day (Monday), July 4th (fixed date), and Labor Day (Monday), shift or extend the series slot to a 4-game wrap-around (e.g., Friday–Monday) so that ALL 30 teams play on these three holidays (no off-days for any team on these dates).

3. Schedule Composition (162 games per team; 2,430 total games):
- Intradivision (52 games): Exactly 13 games vs. each of 4 division opponents.
  * Must be split into four series per opponent: three 3-game series and one 4-game series (7 Home / 6 Away or 6 Home / 7 Away).
- Intraleague Non-Division (64 games): 6 to 7 games per opponent across the 10 other league teams (split into two series per opponent: 3-3 or 3-4).
- Interleague (46 games): Remaining games against opposite-league teams in 3-game series.
- All scheduled series must be either 3 or 4 games.

4. Optimization Constraints (Google OR-Tools CP-SAT):
- Hard Constraints:
  * Balance: Exactly 81 home games and 81 away games per team over the season.
  * Symmetry: Every home series for Team A vs Team B must be paired as an away series for Team B vs Team A in the same slot.
  * No Double-Booking: Each team plays in exactly one series per slot.
  * Divisional Spacing:
    - For every team, at least one series against each of its 4 divisional opponents must occur on or before April 30.
    - For every team, at least one series against each of its 4 divisional opponents must occur on or after September 1.
  * Road Trip & Homestand Lengths:
    - Maximum consecutive away series: 3 series (max 10-12 games).
    - Minimum consecutive away series on a road trip: 2 series (avoid single-series road trips except at season boundaries).
    - Maximum consecutive home series: 3 series (max 10-12 games).
- Soft Objectives (Multi-objective optimization):
  * Minimize total travel distance across all 30 teams (penalty based on distance matrix between consecutive series locations).
  * Maximize the proportion of divisional series placed within the first 4 weeks and final 4 weeks of the season.
  * Minimize isolated single-series homestands.

5. Execution & Output:
- Implement a two-phase solve or hierarchical CP-SAT model to ensure quick convergence (under 3–5 minutes runtime).
- Expand the series matrix into a day-by-day game schedule.
- Output a pandas DataFrame and export to 'mlb_schedule.csv' with columns:

The 2026 MLB schedule was missing some division rivalry matchups both early and late in the season

The 2026 MLB schedule was missing some division rivalry matchups both early and late in the season, so I wanted to see if I could improve on it by building my own version of the schedule for all 30 teams.  Along the way, I learned a lot about what MLB prioritizes in their own scheduling model.

These are the main problems I was trying to solve:
1. Division Rivals: Teams were missing matchups against division rivals both early in the season as well as late in the season.  (e.g.: the first Braves vs Mets series didn't happen until June 12th and the last game was August 12th.)
2. Public Holidays: Teams weren't making full use of public holidays.  4 teams were missing games on Memorial Day.  8 teams were missing games on Labor Day.  2 teams were missing games on Father's Day.
3. Planned Double-headers: Looking at the official 2027 schedule, MLB already has the Padres scheduled for two double-headers vs the Cubs and Colorado, and the season hasn't even started yet.  Every other team has 0 planned double-headers.  This puts those three teams at an unforced disadvantage.
4. Long Sequences: Some teams are playing too many consecutive days without an off-day.  In 2027, there will be 19 teams that have a sequence of 16+ days with no days off, and 7 of those teams have two sequences of 17 consecutive days without a break.  This stresses those teams' depth unnecessarily and creates an unfair playing condition.


Basic scheduling constraints from the CBA can be found [here](https://registrationz.mlbpa.org/pdf/MLB%20Basic%20Agreement%202022-26.pdf), refer to Article V.


Some limitations in this model:
* I'm sure some teams have certain days where they've committed to use the stadium for other events such as concerts, and I'm not taking those into account.  Not sure if there's a central repository that has that.

with teams as (
    select hometeam as team from data
    union
    select awayteam as team from data
), team_seq as (
    select distinct t.team, d.date::date as dt
    from data d
    inner join teams t
    on d.hometeam = t.team or d.awayteam = t.team
), numbered as (
    select team,dt,dt - row_number() over (partition by team order by dt)::int as grp
    from team_seq
), team_ranges as (
select team,min(dt) as range_start,max(dt) as range_end,count(*) as consecutive_days
from numbered
group by team,grp
order by team,range_start
)
select team,count(1) as long_sequences,max(consecutive_days),avg(consecutive_days)
from team_ranges
where consecutive_days>15
group by 1
order by 3 desc
